How a climb goes

To begin, pick a day. Any date since 1940 replays the weather that really hit the mountain, level by level from 4,400 m to the jet stream. The next two weeks use the live forecast.

Then, acclimatise. Your body adapts to the height you spend time at. Go up too fast and the headache comes first, then fluid on the lungs or a swelling brain, and the screen blurs, greys and lags with you.

Above 8,000 metres, oxygen decides everything. Two litres a minute makes the summit feel like 7,450 m. Clip into the fixed lines on the knife edge, because a slip you can't self-arrest is the end.

On the way down, the climb is only half done. Pemba, your Sherpa, will short-rope you if you collapse; Ruth, the expedition leader, will order you down on the radio. The game ends when you reach a tent alive.
